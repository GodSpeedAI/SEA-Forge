# GodSpeed AI | Universal Knowledge Work & FDE Operating Procedure

**Document ID:** GS-FDE-SOP-001 · **Version:** 0.4 (synthesis) · **Date:** 2026-11-12
**Sources:** v0.2 (2026-11-10, `GodSpeed_Universal_FDE_Knowledge_Work_SOP_v0.2.md`, cited `A:line`) and the
earlier narrative SOP (2026-07-01, `godspeed-growth/world/godspeed_ai_fde_sop_v0_3.md`, cited `B:line`).
**Rule of synthesis:** v0.2 governs control, evidence, authority and settlement. The narrative SOP supplies field
artifacts, field lists and discovery heuristics that v0.2 left abstract. Where they conflict, v0.2 wins and the
conflict is listed in §13. Every clause below is numbered so the declared model and the acceptance matrix can
cite it (`SOP §n.m`).

## 1. Purpose and governing rule

1.1 The unit of work is a **Field Case**: a bounded, versioned, purposeful case through which an FDE, delegated
agents, domain specialists and independent observers investigate, intervene, verify, decide and learn (A:11).
1.2 **At every decision, pick the next move that is useful, presently affordable, authorized, executable,
recoverable and independently settleable** (A:13). A plausible option is not an available affordance.
1.3 An observed difference is information, not automatically a defect. It may show a missing distinction, a wrong
implementation, insufficient evidence, or a useful adaptation. Keep those open until evidence attributes it (A:15).
1.4 Required outcome: a defensible case disposition, inspectable evidence, and an explicit account of what the
experience changes, or does not change, for later work (A:17).
1.5 Four field tests apply to any proposed loop (B:2370-2373): no authority means unsafe; no evidence means not
trusted; no residue means it does not compound; no better next problem means it did not develop.

## 2. Four layers

| Layer | Job | Question |
|---|---|---|
| Strategy (Playing to Win, ODI/JTBD) | choose customer, problem, desired progress, trade-offs, value, abandonment conditions | which outcome is worth pursuing, for whom, why now |
| Navigation (Golden Key: Listen → Aim → Move → Claim) | recurs at every consequential decision | what next, and what did reality establish |
| Delivery (ten activities, §6) | repertoire of work; overlapping, repeatable, omittable | what work creates or tests the next distinction |
| Control (nine evidence gates, §8) | independent predicates on the case, not a pipeline | what evidence permits the next commitment |

2.1 A Field Case is an operational projection of case navigation, not a new ontology primitive. An **episode** is
a bounded inquiry or intervention bound to a predeclared evaluation reference. A **Case Assignment** is a
delegated unit of work with its own authority, scope, budget, evidence and handoff (A:30).
2.2 Vocabulary fixed by this version (resolves §13 conflicts): **framing** is the map the customer or team is
currently using (the narrative SOP's "projection"); **projection** is a derived view of a model or case;
**case horizon** is the edge of what the observer can presently notice, afford, prove, govern and settle (B:733),
distinct from the Horizon product; **residual** is an unresolved difference; **residue** is what survives a
settled episode for reuse (B:765).

## 3. Roles, separation and component responsibilities

3.1 Roles (A:34): FDE/case lead (framing, coordination, evidence coverage, recommendation); sponsor or GodSpeed
owner (direction, final acceptance); criterion author (what counts, before an evaluated episode); execution
actors (humans, agents, programs); independent observer/verifier; domain and policy custodians (changes to
meaning and authority). DE and ADE specialists own substrates, integrations, agents and evaluations.
3.2 Separation (A:36): one person may hold several roles where stakes allow, but the evaluated executor never
rewrites frozen criteria and never attests its own independent acceptance. In a one-person case the criterion
is frozen before execution and independent settlement is performed by a fresh verifier (human or a fresh-context
agent with no access to the executor's reasoning), recorded as such.
3.3 The FDE owns (B:350-364): problem discovery, problem-class identification, vocabulary capture, case-horizon
reading, affordance mapping, authority-surface and evidence-surface discovery, smallest-settlement
identification, case documentation. The FDE does not own implementation detail or delivery mechanics (B:388-404).
3.4 Components (A:38-48, authoritative over B:791-837):

| Component | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| DomainForge / `.sea` | versioned domain meaning and Interaction Domain Models |
| SEA-Forge / Casework | governed case and action authority, policy, delegation, approval, obligations |
| Cognate | durable runtime, capabilities, events, projections, continuations |
| Horizon | human-facing world and case navigation, journal, affordance inspection, decisions, comparisons; presents, never decides |
| Cognate Workbench | agent work environment: assignments, sessions, deep-work execution linked to case and evidence |
| RealityTrace / SXR | pre-action expectations, observations, typed differences, provenance, residuals, settlement evidence |
| Gauntlet / SWE_SEED | bounded difficult execution and software verification against frozen references |
| Context Kernel | scoped retrieval with citations; reference material, not canonical truth |
| GodSpeed-Agent | developmental layer over settlement history; proposes, never authorizes |

3.5 Model provider does not determine authority or evidence. EGM review counts as independent only with
genuinely fresh reviewer contexts (A:50).

## 4. Opening or resuming a Field Case

4.1 The FDE opens or retrieves the case in Horizon and confirms it against the authoritative case record (A:54).
4.2 Minimum case content (A:56-60, extended with B:2160-2215). Fields marked † come from the narrative SOP.

| Group | Fields |
|---|---|
| Purpose and outcome | owner/sponsor; signal† (what prompted the case); stated problem†; adopted problem† (what behaviour shows is actually being solved); problem class†; JTBD/outcome; business significance; success direction; non-goals; constraints; decision this work can unlock |
| Framing† | current framing; what it reveals; what it hides; better question; better problem if successful |
| World and boundary | `world_ref` and version; checkpoint; people and systems; observed conditions; statements needing confirmation; known unknowns; interfaces; time horizon |
| Observer and authority | case lead; criterion owner; delegated actors; independent verifier; settlement and disposition authority; risk and external-effect limits; authority surface† (who may approve, block, escalate, modify, execute) |
| Current horizon | evidence and decisions; contested distinctions; hypotheses; possible next moves; blocked moves with reason; available and missing affordances†; budgets (time, attention, money, risk, recovery); ignored payment†; outstanding obligations |
| Continuity | previous episode; worktree/session/artifact links; open questions; current decision; next affordable move |

4.3 A reopened case must be continuable without the previous worker's memory. Journal entries, notes and
transcripts support the case; they never substitute for source-owned state, decisions or evidence (A:62).
4.4 Before substantive work make the smallest useful strategy decision: where to play, how to win, which outcome
is underserved, which assumption is tested, what would cause abandonment. Revisit when evidence changes it (A:64).
4.5 Intake heuristics (B:506-520, 1939-2007): listen for differences between claim and evidence, policy and
behaviour, access and capability, output and settlement, official and shadow process. Record stated versus
revealed problem.

## 5. Payment

5.1 Every candidate move is priced in separate currencies, never one blended score: time, attention, money, risk,
recovery (A:59, A:91). 5.2 Price the non-obvious items explicitly (B:574-590): data cleanup, authority decisions,
policy changes, human-in-the-loop design, evaluation, operational trust. 5.3 Actual payment is measured and
recorded per episode; an unmeasured currency is shown as `unknown`, never zero.

## 6. Delivery activities (not chronological)

| Activity | Action | Durable output |
|---|---|---|
| Embed | establish sponsor decision, setting, users, incentives, constraints, permission; learn the work vocabulary | purpose, stakeholder/authority map, outcome hypothesis, approved observation access |
| Observe | examine work-as-done: exceptions, workarounds, interruptions, handoffs, statement/operation gaps | source-grounded observations, chronology, contested claims, unknowns, baseline |
| Map | reconstruct interactions, transitions, decision points, costs, failure paths, causal hypotheses; apply ODI/JTBD | interaction map, outcome measures, smallest consequential experiment candidates |
| Artifactize | capture minimum reviewable meaning in a validated IDM/`.sea` with source mappings and unknowns | versioned semantic world, interpretation provenance, model identity |
| Prototype | exercise the smallest admitted path that distinguishes hypotheses or produces value | frozen hypothesis, bounded slice, real inputs/outputs, trace identifiers |
| Validate | compare against predeclared expectations; challenge implementation, representation, attribution, recovery | RealityTrace evidence, verified/failed/unresolved results, reviewer disposition |
| Deploy | bind to real systems, actors, permissions, capacity, monitoring, reversibility | authorized activation record, operational owner, recovery procedure |
| Instrument | semantic observation throughout; correlate action to meaning, authority, cost, evidence, consequence | durable semantic exhaust, observed model, burden measures, coverage gaps |
| Learn | diagnose residuals: observation, reference, inference, implementation, or structure; test corrections | diagnostic account, retained residuals, versioned proposals, subsequent-episode tests |
| Compound | preserve what later work can reuse and test its portability | candidate reusable assets, variation/transfer evidence, productization decision |

6.1 Instrumentation starts as soon as an episode needs evidence; learning starts with the first unexpected
observation (A:83). 6.2 Episode sizing (B:2087-2097): prefer one team, one recurring problem, one loop, one
authority surface, one evidence trail. 6.3 Residue to capture (B:765): traces, examples, corrected outputs,
evaluation cases, domain rules, decisions, policies, lessons.

## 7. The bounded decision and episode procedure

7.1 **Listen.** Read the case, `world_ref`, checkpoint, decision history and reliable observations. Separate direct
observation, interpretation, disputed claim and missing evidence. Ask the cheapest question likely to change the
next action (A:89).
7.2 **Aim.** Compare candidate paths on seven dimensions: visibility, reach, payment, executable capability,
authority, recovery, settlement access. Show blocked or unknown possibilities as such. For an evaluated path fix
*before execution*: versioned intention, expected change, invariants, predicted irrelevancies, failure and success
regions, falsifiers, evidence source, authorized decision-maker. Freeze the episode reference and its hash. An
exploratory observation is labelled exploratory at the time, never relabelled a passing test (A:91).
7.3 **Move.** Bind the assignment to case, world, criterion, authority and budget. Obtain SEA-Forge authorization
at the action boundary for consequential actions. Route deep agent work through Cognate/Workbench and, where
needed, Gauntlet and SWE_SEED. Capture execution, cost, external effects, denials and interruptions. Within-
reference corrections are allowed; a change to success criteria, governing meaning or policy starts a new
authorized evaluation version (A:93).
7.4 Assignment brief (B:2238-2264): problem the agent may adopt; domain meaning required; context sources
allowed; tools allowed; authority before action; evidence after action; human judgment points; escalation
conditions; settlement criteria; residue captured.
7.5 **Claim.** RealityTrace links the frozen expectation to independent observation. Classify the epistemic result
**S** established success, **F** established failure, **U** unresolved, **C** contradictory. Record settlement
reliability and source independence separately. The authorized actor chooses **close-satisfied, close-failed,
continue, revise, escalate or suspend**. Update case, residuals, next affordances, handoff. A completed tool call
is not a settled outcome; a settled episode is not a closed case or an earned capability (A:95).
7.6 Four status axes stay distinct (A:97): case lifecycle `open | suspended | closed`; execution `planned |
running | completed | failed | cancelled`; epistemic result `S | F | U | C`; authorized disposition. Mapping:
`close-satisfied` and `close-failed` set lifecycle `closed`; `suspend` sets `suspended`; `continue`, `revise`,
`escalate` leave it `open`.
7.7 Reliability dimensions, applied only where the evaluation contract sets thresholds (A:97): **T** timeliness,
**A** attribution, **I** incentive alignment, **K** corroboration, **D** debt visibility, **X** externality
detection, **N** non-adversariality.

## 8. Nine evidence gates

8.1 A gate is `met | blocked | unassessed | not_applicable` (with authorized rationale). Not applicable never
waives a hard governance condition. Reassess when supporting evidence changes. No automatic advance (A:101, 115).

| Gate | Commitment earned | Minimum evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Choose Problem | invest in discovery | sponsor-owned need, JTBD outcome, plausible value, scope |
| Discover Reality | select an intervention or inquiry | work-as-done observations, baseline, exceptions, competing interpretations, missing knowledge |
| Allocate Authority | delegate or authorize consequential work | roles, scope, policy, approval, recovery ownership, permission boundaries |
| Prove Value | claim pilot benefit | frozen hypothesis and evaluator, representative observation, baseline comparison, measured payment |
| Prove Reliability | expand reliance | independent repeated settlement under variation, edge cases, recovery, known limits |
| Integrate/Govern | operate in the intended environment | approved connections, authority enforcement, observability, rollback, security, operational owner |
| Earn Trust | expect informed human use | operator understanding, informed adoption, escalation and recovery behaviour |
| Measure | claim attributable improvement | benefit and all payment currencies, debt, externalities, confounders, limits |
| Productize | offer a reusable capability | versioned pattern, transfer hypothesis, repeatability/variation/recovery evidence, business case |

8.2 A case may end with the evidenced decision not to deploy, or reopen discovery after a promising pilot.

## 9. Error, structural revision and developmental memory

9.1 Two comparisons, never merged (A:121-122): **episode comparison** (frozen expected versus observed, one
bounded act) and **structural comparison** (versioned declared IDM versus an observed interaction representation
reconstructed from traceable events across episodes, preserving frequency, ordering, provenance, missing coverage
and contextual variation). Never infer a whole organization from a partial sample.
9.2 Diagnose a consequential difference as: observation/instrumentation problem, mistaken reference, unsupported
inference, implementation failure, contextual variation, or inadequate declared structure (A:124).
9.3 Recurrence triggers for structural review (B:1757-1764): same issue after handoffs, after local fixes, same
audit finding, a workaround made permanent.
9.4 A structural revision records: candidate semantic change, rationale, retained invariants, migration and
recovery path, held-out tests, authority, subsequent episode. A new `world_ref` is promoted only through the
governing process; old evidence stays attached to the version it was judged against (A:126).
9.5 Developmental memory states what the next observer can now distinguish, choose, verify, recover from or
accomplish. A reusable asset is a candidate until it survives variation and transfer. Divergence counts need not
fall as representation improves (A:128).

## 10. Continuity, handoff and disposition

10.1 Case continuation, derived from source-owned records (A:134-138): case ID, owner, purpose, current decision;
pinned world and checkpoint; gate evidence; frozen or pending episodes; exact authority/approval state; disputed
claims; latest observations with RealityTrace references, unknown and contradictory evidence exposed; active
assignments and trajectories, artifact locations, verified commands, remaining budgets; next affordable action or
blocker, who can authorize it, what would settle it.
10.2 Receiver-specific handoff (B:326, 328). To delivery engineering: problem class, actors, constraints, rhythms,
evidence, decisions, artifacts, smallest settlement. To agent engineering: domain vocabulary, task boundaries,
authority surfaces, agent-adoptable questions, evaluation candidates, context needs, evidence requirements.
10.3 Close only by authorized disposition, with provenance, economic/outcome account, remaining liabilities and
recoverability. Closure is an organizational decision, not universal certainty (A:140).
10.4 The reusable handoff is: case summary, semantic references, evidence bundle, decision record, open residuals,
qualified reusable artifacts, usable by a different FDE (A:142).

## 11. Acceptance of this SOP: first internal Field Case

Use this SOP as declared practice v0.4, freeze its expectation and model hash, then run one bounded real GodSpeed
assignment through it. A second operator or independent verifier must be able to (A:150-157):
1. reconstruct or validate the initial IDM from this SOP and its source evidence;
2. resume the case from authoritative records and select a real or blocked affordance;
3. run a bounded governed inquiry against an expectation frozen beforehand and inspect execution and evidence;
4. derive an observed interaction representation, compare it with the declared version, classify at least one difference or evidence gap;
5. independently settle an episode, authorize its disposition, preserve the original reference and all negative or unresolved evidence;
6. where justified, validate and authorize a candidate model and test a subsequent episode under it; where not, retain the divergence and the rationale;
7. inspect the same case, differences, authority and evidence through Horizon and programmatic records without inconsistent verdicts;
8. transfer the templates and instrumentation to a second bounded situation without rewriting the core evidence mechanism.

Outcome per requirement: PROVEN, PARTIAL or BLOCKED, with runnable commands and trace/model/version references.
One passing case proves the bounded mechanism only. First external transfer candidate: a tax professional's
pre-filing statutory verification and evidence-binding workflow; customer evidence and professional acceptance
are obtained separately (A:159).

## 12. Records and owners

| Record | Canonical owner |
|---|---|
| Case and assignments | Casework/Cognate case and event interfaces; Horizon is the operator view |
| Declared interaction model | validated `.sea` in the owning domain's `.sea/interaction/`, with model hash and source translation |
| Frozen episode reference | evaluation and execution contract fixed before the act; linked from case events and RealityTrace |
| Observed traces and comparisons | RealityTrace records and derivable observed representation; the case carries references, not a copy |
| Decision, review, developmental residue | attributable authorization/acceptance, case decisions, candidate revision or reusable artifact |
| Proof report | reproduction commands, source hashes, positive and adversarial results, uncertainty, verifier identity |

Templates and YAML serializations are convenience projections; they never replace validated `.sea`, runtime
contracts or source-owned histories (A:174).

| Topic | Narrative SOP | Resolution |

**Standing criterion (A:178):** the SOP is useful if it gets GodSpeed to a warranted decision faster, makes real
work recoverable and independently verifiable, and preserves distinctions that improve later decisions. Its value
is established by consequences, not by conformance to its own prose.
